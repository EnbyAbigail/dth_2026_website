## The Benefits of Open Source

Open source can to some degree be considered the baseline for all software at the conception of it and at the inception of computer programming. A piece of software becomes closed source with the involvement of corporate and financial interests. Many independent programmers, or employed ones who enjoy the sport of it, decide that their source code should be open for others to view and work on. This was the decision made by Linus Torvalds when he made Linux and changed the world of operating systems utilizing open source.

Linux being open source is a great place to start when discussing the advantages of open source, as it has allowed users to find operating systems which appeal to them, or if they have specific needs they were able to grab the reins and create or modify their own version. Open source operating systems are also usually created through crowdfunding or passion, utilizing volunteers, and most Linux OS are free to access, unlike mainstream closed source ones such as Windows or MacOS. In an open source OS, code can be viewed and modified, this also helps corporations be more efficient and avoid vendor lock-in (Pratt 2022; Morgan 2007).

Open source software is collaborative in nature, as other users are able to branch out from your work and contribute with their own additions, edits and ideas, which can ultimately lead to rapid improvements that closed source may never achieve (Mangnani 2023). It is also considered far more reliable and safe than most closed source by IT leaders (Pratt 2022).
